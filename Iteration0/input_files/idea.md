**Title: Quantifying the RG Flow of Fractional Diffusion: From Kraichnan Idealization to Intermittent Kolmogorov Turbulence**

This study rigorously maps the renormalization group (RG) flow of the effective fractional diffusion exponent $\alpha_{eff}(\tau)$ by analyzing the transition from short-time Gaussian behavior to the long-time Lévy-stable fixed point $\alpha = 2/\xi$. We utilize the Kraichnan tracer datasets ($1.5 \le \xi \le 1.8$) to extract the crossover time $\tau_c$ as a function of spectral roughness $\xi$ and confirm the convergence of the characteristic function $\phi(k, \tau)$ to the form $\exp(-D_\alpha |k|^\alpha \tau)$. We then compute the intermittency-corrected spectral exponents $\xi(\mu) = 1 + \zeta_2(\mu)$ from the log-normal cascade datasets ($\mu = 0.0, 0.15, 0.28$) to provide a precise theoretical prediction for the fractional index in physical turbulence. By applying this $\xi(\mu)$ mapping to the Kolmogorov-synthetic tracer trajectories, we test whether non-white-in-time velocity fields satisfy the predicted relation $\alpha = 2/(1+\zeta_2)$. This approach isolates the impact of intermittency on anomalous transport, establishing whether the fractional diffusion operator remains a robust descriptor of tracer statistics when the Kraichnan white-noise assumption is relaxed.
